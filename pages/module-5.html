Module 5

Applications and Challenges

Explore where Big Data is used and why privacy, security, and fairness matter.

Learning Objectives

  • Identify Big Data applications in several industries.
  • Explain privacy, security, and bias concerns.
  • Evaluate a scenario using responsible data practices.

Lesson Content

Big Data is used in many areas of daily life. Businesses use it to understand customers, healthcare systems use it to study patient patterns, social media platforms use it to recommend content, and transportation systems use it to improve routes and safety.

These uses can be helpful, but Big Data also creates challenges. Privacy matters because data can reveal personal habits and locations. Security matters because large data collections can become targets. Bias matters because unfair or incomplete data can lead to unfair results.

In healthcare, Big Data can help identify disease patterns or improve hospital scheduling. In business, it can help stores predict demand and avoid running out of popular products. In education, it can show which lessons students repeat or where quiz scores drop. In each case, the benefit depends on collecting the right data and using it carefully.

Responsible data use means asking clear questions before collecting information: What problem are we solving? What data is actually needed? Who could be harmed if the data is exposed or misunderstood? A good system should protect personal information, explain how data is used, and avoid decisions that unfairly affect a group of people.

Scenario

A school app wants to collect study time, quiz scores, and location data. Some data may help improve learning, but the team must ask what is necessary, what should stay private, and how students are protected.

Responsible Design Checklist

Strong Big Data projects limit unnecessary data, protect sensitive details, check results for bias, and give users clear information about how their data supports the service.

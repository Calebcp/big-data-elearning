Module 1

Introduction to Big Data

Placeholder lesson page for the Writer to add final Big Data introduction content.

Learning Objectives

  • Define Big Data in beginner-friendly language.
  • Identify simple examples of Big Data in daily life.
  • Explain why data size, speed, and usefulness matter.

Lesson Content

Big Data means data that is too large, too fast, or too complex for ordinary tools to handle easily. A small class spreadsheet is data, but millions of app clicks, video views, purchases, sensor readings, or map locations can become Big Data because there is so much information arriving all the time.

The important idea is not only size. Big Data becomes useful when people can find patterns in it. A shopping website may study what customers view and buy. A music app may recommend songs based on listening habits. A city may use traffic data to understand busy roads and improve transportation.

Quick Example

One student's playlist history is normal data. Millions of students streaming songs every minute across many countries is closer to Big Data.

Interactive Activity

Knowledge Check

Question: Why is Big Data not only about the amount of data?

Answer idea: Speed, complexity, variety, and usefulness also matter.
